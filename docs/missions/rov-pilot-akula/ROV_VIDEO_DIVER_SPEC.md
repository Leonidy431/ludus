## Introduction

This section includes suggestions on how to use this guide, an overview of
course philosophy and goals.

### How to Use this Guide

This guide speaks to you, the Remote Operated Video Diver Specialty
Instructor. The guide contains four sections – the first contains standards
specific to this course, the second contains knowledge development
presentations, the third considers confined water training, and the fourth
details the open water dives.

All required standards, learning objectives, activities, and performance
requirements specific to the Remote Operated Video Diver course appear in
boldface print. The boldface assists you in easily identifying those
requirements that you must adhere to when you conduct the course. Items not
in boldface print are recommendations for your information and
consideration. General course standards applicable to all PADI courses are
located in the General Standards and Procedures section of your PADI
Instructor Manual.

### Course Overview and Goals

The purpose of the PADI Remote Operated Video Diver Specialty Diver Course
is to assist Underwater Archeologists with methods of searching, research,
discovery and preservation of monuments of the past under water.

The goals of Remote Operated Video Diver raining are:

- To know the equipment that help to conduct underwater missions;
- To know methods of piloting REMOTE OPERATED VIDEO equipment.
- Ensure safety of divers, yours safety and legality of the research;
- How to map the dive site;
- To know how to prepare REMOTE OPERATED VIDEO at surface;
- Features of REMOTE OPERATED VIDEO underwater navigation for underwater
  research;

### Course Flow Options

This course contains knowledge development and 2 open water training dives.
When possible, you should conduct the knowledge development session before
any confined water training. The confined water skill development session
must precede the open water training dives.

There are 2 open water dives to complete. You may rearrange skill sequences
within each dive; however, the sequence of dives must stay intact. You may
add more dives as necessary to meet student divers' needs. Organize your
course to incorporate environment friendly techniques throughout each dive,
to accommodate student diver learning style, logistical needs, and your
sequencing preferences.

### Instructor Prerequisites

To qualify to teach the Remote Operated Video Diver course, an individual
must be a Teaching status PADI OWSI or higher. PADI Instructors may apply
for the Diver Distinctive Specialty Instructor rating after completing a
Specialty Instructor Training course with a PADI Course Director, or by
Remote Operated Video or by providing proof of experience and applying
directly to PADI. For further detail, reference Membership Standards in the
General Standards and Procedures section of your PADI Instructor Manual.

### Student Diver Prerequisites

By the start of the course, a diver must be:

1. Certified as a PADI Advanced Open Water Diver or have a qualifying
   certification from another training organization
2. Be at least 18 years.

### Supervision and Ratios

**Open Water Dives**

A Teaching status PADI Remote Operated Video Diver Specialty Instructor must
be present and in direct control of all activities and must ensure that all
performance requirements are met.

The ratio for confined and open water dives is 3 student divers per
instructor (3:1).

### Site, Depths, and Hours

**Site**

Choose sites with conditions and environments suitable for completing
requirements. Shallow dives will pRemoted Operated Video ide divers with
more time to complete tasks. Use different open water dive sites, if
possible, to give students divers experience in dealing with a variety of
environmental conditions (incorporate environment friendly techniques
throughout each dive) and logistical challenges.

**Depths**

- 6-12 metres/20-40 feet recommended
- 30 meters / 100 feet limit

**Hours**

The PADI Remote Operated Video Diver course includes 2 open water dives.
Conduct dives During daylight hours between sunrise and sunset. The minimum
number of recommended hours is 10 hours.

### Assessment Standards

The student diver must demonstrate accurate and adequate knowledge during
the open water dives and must perform all skills (procedures and motor
skills) fluidly, with little difficulty, in a manner that demonstrates
minimal or no stress.

### Certification Requirements and Procedures

By the completion of the course, student divers must complete all
performance requirements for Remote Operated Video Diver Open Water Dives
One and Two.

The instructor certifying the student diver must ensure that all
certification requirements have been met. The certifying instructor obtains
a Remote Operated Video Diver certification by submitting a completed,
signed PIC to the appropriate PADI office.

## Section Four: Open Water Dives

### General Open Water Considerations

1. Involve student divers in dive-planning activities. Give special
   attention to student diver anxiety and stress levels, in addition to
   student diver equipment preparedness.
2. Conduct a thorough briefing. The better the briefing, the more smoothly
   the night dive will proceed. Assign buddy teams according to ability
   (weak with strong) and establish a check-in/check-out procedure.
3. Assign logistical duties to staff and review emergency protocols.
4. Remind divers to familiarize themselves with their buddies' equipment.
5. Evaluate diver's thermal protection for appropriateness for the dive
   site and expected conditions.
6. Make yourself available to answer questions during equipment assembly,
   safety checks and gear-up.
7. Explain the related documents including map of the site and images from
   Side-scan sonar during underwater searches.
8. Know how to ensure your safety and legality of the local or
   international research.
